# TRAJECTORY

Status: **normative target specification**

## Definition

```text
SIMPLEPROGRAM + SEED -> TRAJECTORY
```

A Trajectory is one definite SimpleProgram paired with one definite compatible
Seed. In dynamical-systems language, it is one dynamics paired with one initial
condition.

```python
Trajectory(
    program=simple_program,
    seed=seed,
)
```

The Trajectory denotes the evolution implied by that pairing whether or not
later states have been materialized. For deterministic dynamics this is one
path. How the term is realized for branching, stochastic, continuous, or
event-driven dynamics remains part of the deferred rollout design.

## Fields

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `SIMPLEPROGRAM` | The reusable dynamics |
| `SEED` | The exact initial state |

No third semantic field is required.

## Where Concrete Extent Becomes Known

Trajectory is the first level at which a shape-polymorphic SimpleProgram and a
concrete Seed meet.

For a finite Space whose extent is supplied by Seed:

```text
finite t+2D periodic SPACE
    + 5x5 SEED
    -> 5x5 TRAJECTORY

the same SPACE
    + 11x11 SEED
    -> 11x11 TRAJECTORY
```

The SimpleProgram is unchanged. The Trajectories differ because their initial
conditions and concrete extents differ.

For an infinite Space, Seed support is only the initially realized or
non-default portion. It does not turn the infinite Space into a finite world.
Space decides how the Seed's support is interpreted.

## Compatibility

A SimpleProgram and Seed may form a Trajectory when:

- the Seed's coordinate rank and support are admitted by Space;
- the Seed's concrete extent satisfies Space's extent law;
- every Seed value is admitted by Alphabet; and
- the Seed is a complete initial state under the representation chosen for
  that Space, including any explicit default needed for an infinite support.

Compatibility is a relation between independently generated values. Seeds are
not owned or generated by one SimpleProgram.

One Seed may pair with many SimplePrograms. For example, the same binary
`11x11` Seed can pair with periodic, fixed-zero, and fixed-one boundary
variants, provided the relevant Alphabets admit its values.

## Meaning

A Trajectory identifies **which path** is under discussion. It does not say how
much of that path to compute now.

The evolution begins with Seed at its explicit initial time, ordinarily
`t=0`. For ordinary discrete dynamics, later states occupy new time
coordinates. Earlier coordinates remain immutable; logical persistence means
copying a value into a later time slice, not updating an old coordinate.

## Non-Responsibilities

Trajectory does not own:

- a rollout step count, duration, depth, or stopping limit;
- CPU, GPU, memory, timeout, or solver resources;
- materialized Episode arrays;
- downstream selection or batching policy;
- serialization, tokenization, masks, or dataset splits; or
- taxonomy metadata unrelated to semantic identity.

Those concerns either belong to the later rollout design or remain downstream.

## Identity

Trajectory identity is determined by its SimpleProgram and Seed:

```text
same SIMPLEPROGRAM + different SEED -> different TRAJECTORY
different SIMPLEPROGRAM + same SEED -> different TRAJECTORY
```

Changing only a future rollout limit does not select another Trajectory. It
changes what an eventual rollout asks to realize from the same path.

## Formation from Plural Sources

If a Preset generates `SIMPLEPROGRAMS` and a Seed source generates `SEEDS`,
the possible Trajectories are their compatible pairings:

\[
\mathrm{TRAJECTORIES}
=
\{(P,S)\in\mathrm{SIMPLEPROGRAMS}\times\mathrm{SEEDS}
\mid S\text{ is compatible with }P\}.
\]

This need not be materialized as a full Cartesian product. A caller may
enumerate, sample, filter, or select pairings as its application requires.

## Relationship to Rollout

Only the following arrow is settled:

```text
rollout(TRAJECTORY, RESOURCES/LIMIT) -> EPISODE
```

The rollout contract is deliberately unresolved. See [EPISODE.md](EPISODE.md).
