## Book Excerpts

### E1 — T44/T45 categorical boundary

- Provenance: `BOOK:2018-2024`, strict main.
- Establishes: T44 retains discrete cells/steps; T45 posits values at every space/time point and specifies derivative relations without explicit steps.

> “But there is nevertheless much discreteness that remains: for a continuous cellular automaton is still made up of discrete cells that are updated in discrete time steps.”

> “The basic idea is to imagine that a quantity such as gray level can be set up to vary continuously in space and time. And what this means is that instead of just having gray levels in discrete cells at discrete time steps, one supposes that there exists a definite gray level at absolutely every point in space and every moment in time—as if one took the limit of an infinite collection of cells and time steps, with each cell being an infinitesimal size, and each time step lasting an infinitesimal time.”

> “Having no explicit time steps to work with, one must instead just specify the rate at which the gray level changes with time at every point in space. And typically one gives this rate as a simple formula that depends on the gray level at each point in space, and on the rate at which that gray level changes with position.”

### E13 — PDE as constraint and solution set

- Provenance: `BOOK:13357`, native Notes.

> “Unlike systems such as cellular automata, PDEs do not have a built-in notion of "evolution" or "time". > “So-called hyperbolic equations (such as the wave equation, the sine-Gordon equation and my equation) work a little like cellular automata in that in at least one dimension information can propagate only at a limited speed, say c. > “In other PDEs, such as so-called elliptic ones, there is no such limit on the rate of information propagation, and as a result, it is immediately necessary to know values of u[t, x] at all x, and on the boundaries of the region, in order to determine u[t, x] for any t > 0.”

### E16 — Numerical discretization is not the PDE

- Provenance: `BOOK:13401-13411`, native Notes.
- Establishes: finite difference creates discrete space/time cells resembling continuous CA; the reported Courant threshold separates the shown behaviors; all numerical methods discretize and can create artifacts.

> “To find numerical solutions to PDEs on a digital computer one has no choice but to make approximations. In the typical case of the finite difference method one sets up a system with discrete cells in space and time that is much like a continuous cellular automaton, and then hopes that when the cells in this system are made small enough its behavior will be close to that of the continuous PDE.”

> “So long as the so-called Courant condition dt/dx < 1/2 is satisfied, the results are correct. But when dt/dx is made larger, an instability develops, and the discrete approximation yields completely different results from the continuous PDE.”

> “All however ultimately involve discretization, and can suffer from difficulties that are similar—though often more insidious—to those for finite differences.”

> “And indeed in practice it is often difficult to tell whether complexity that is seen is actually a consequence of the underlying PDE, or is instead merely a reflection of the discretization procedure.”

> “For equations where one can come at least close to having explicit algebraic formulas for solutions, it has often been possible to prove that a certain discretization procedure will yield correct results. But when the form of the true solution is more complicated, such proofs are typically impossible.”

### E21 — Stability, dispersion, and conservation are properties

- Provenance: `BOOK:15518,16156-16157`, supporting Notes.
- Establishes: linear stability/dispersion analysis and local/global conservation laws are diagnostics/properties, not solution identity.

> “A standard approach to partial differential equations (PDEs) used for more than a century is so-called linear stability analysis, in which one assumes that small fluctuations around some kind of basic solution can be treated as a superposition of waves of the form  $Exp[ikx]Exp[i\omega t]$ . And at least in a linear approximation any given PDE then typically implies that  $\omega$ is connected to the wavenumber k by a so-called dispersion relation, which often has a simple algebraic form.”

> “For some kthis yields a value of  $\omega$  that is real—corresponding to an ordinary wave that maintains the same amplitude. But for some k one often finds that  $\omega$  has an imaginary part. The most common case  $Im[\omega] > 0$  yields exponential damping. But particularly when the original PDE is nonlinear one often finds that  $Im[\omega] < 0$  for some range of k—implying an instability which causes modes with certain spatial wavelengths to grow. The mode with the most negative  $Im[\omega]$  will grow fastest, potentially leading to repetitive behavior that shows a particular dominant spatial wavelength.”

> “In the early 1960s it was discovered that certain nonlinear PDEs support an infinite number of distinct conserved quantities, associated with so-called integrability and the presence of solitons. Systematic methods now exist to find conserved quantities that are given by integrals of polynomials of any given degree in the dependent variables and their derivatives. Most randomly chosen PDEs appear, however, to have no such conserved quantities.”

> “Whenever a system like a cellular automaton (or PDE) has a global conserved quantity there must always be a local conservation law which expresses the fact that every point in the system the total flux of the conserved quantity into a particular region must equal the rate of increase of the quantity inside it.”

```text
nonlinear Schrodinger:    i*u_t = -u_xx + 4*Abs[u]^2*u
Kuramoto-Sivashinsky:     u_t = -u_xx - (1/2)*u_xxxx + (u_x)^2
```

> “Note that Abs[u] is plotted in the second picture, while for the last equation a common less symmetrical form replaces the last term by  $u[t, x] \partial_x u[t, x].$”

## Construction Model

### Immutable mathematical definition

```text
DifferentialEquationSpec {
  independent_variables: ordered tuple of at least two distinct ContinuousVariable[Real],
  dependent_field: FieldSymbol[real_scalar | complex_scalar | fixed_real_vector(n)],
  parameters: ordered BoundParameter[ExactOrDeclaredScalar | FixedMatrixValue],
  equation: Equal(lhs,rhs),
  operator_registry_version
}

PDEProblem {
  equation_id,
  continuous_region,
  side_data: ordered conditions on named loci/hypersurfaces/identifications,
  solution_concept: Classical,
  regularity: DifferentialRegularity
}

ClosedBoundExpression {
  binders: ordered distinct ContinuousVariable references,
  codomain: real_scalar | complex_scalar | fixed_real_vector(n),
  body: closed structural expression over exactly those binders and declared parameters,
  definition_domain,
  branches_and_partiality,
  primitive_registry_version
}

CandidateFieldDefinition {
  problem_id,
  value: ClosedBoundExpression whose binders exactly match the equation variables
         and whose codomain exactly matches the dependent field
}

TraceRelation {
  locus_parameterization_id,
  trace_target: field-or-component plus derivative multi-index,
  rhs: ClosedBoundExpression whose binders exactly match the locus parameters
       and whose codomain matches the trace target
}

EquationClassificationClaim {
  equation_id, classification, analysis_scope, method_version,
  replayable_premises_or_certificate, proof_strength
}

SideDataAdmissibilityClaim {
  problem_id, ordered_side_data_ids, locus_analysis_ids,
  classification_claim_ids, preset_id_or_method_version,
  replayable_premises_or_certificate, outcome
}

Solutions(problem) = {
  functions in the declared solution class satisfying every equation on the
  interior and every side condition on its declared locus
}
```

Equation-family identity excludes region/data/concept. Problem identity includes them. A scalar formula that happens to satisfy a problem is a solution witness, not the equation or full solution set.

### Continuous variables, fields, and domains

- A PDE record binds at least two distinct ordered real independent variables; a one-variable differential equation is rejected as the ODE category. Variable roles remain explicit only where needed. `t` is not automatically privileged; a time role is required for an IVP/flow query.
- The strict core has one real scalar dependent field over a finite ordered tuple of real coordinates. Notes support a complex-scalar nonlinear-Schrodinger sibling and one fixed two-component reaction-diffusion field with explicit matrix operations. The field declaration therefore admits real scalar, complex scalar, or validated fixed real vector codomain.
- Maxwell, Einstein, Schrodinger, and Hodgkin-Huxley are application examples, not enough evidence to invent arbitrary named-field, tensor, bundle, gauge, manifold, or coordinate-chart schemas. A future system compositor may add those only after direct construction evidence and tests; v1 uses one possibly vector-valued field declaration.
- A continuous region is structural mathematical geometry—interval/product/whole Euclidean space or a later closed region predicate/stratification—not a finite integer grid.
- Named boundary strata, initial hypersurfaces, and paired periodic faces/loci are explicit. Infinite space has no ghost-cell policy.
- Strict whole-line Gaussian interpretation is a labeled inference from formulas and the `(-infinity,infinity)` energy context, not a silently asserted finite segment.

### Closed differential syntax

- Reuse T41 exact/declared scalar literals, algebra, elementary/special primitives, domains, and branch/version records. T41's unary function wrapper is not reused as a PDE solution.
- Add bound `IndependentVarRef`, `FieldRef(field,arguments)`, and `PartialDerivative(expression,multi_index)` nodes. Multi-indices name bound variables and nonnegative orders; arity, variable binding, component types, and equation dimensions are validated.
- Add exact/declared fixed matrix literals/parameters and closed dimension-checked matrix-vector product, fixed-vector construction, component selection, and vector algebra. This is the direct `c={u,v}`, `d partial_xx c + m c` obligation, not general tensor syntax.
- The equation is a structural scalar/vector `Equal(lhs,rhs)` record over its bound field and parameters. Fixed vector/matrix dimensions are validated. It cannot contain callbacks, strings, `eval`, host CAS objects, or trusted derivative functions.
- `ClosedBoundExpression` is the single reusable carrier for a typed multivariate body, its ordered binders, definition domain, branches/partiality, and primitive version. A candidate field uses the complete equation-variable binder and full field codomain. A trace right-hand side instead uses exactly the parameters of its named locus and may target one field component. This avoids forcing boundary data into the shape of a full candidate solution while retaining the same closure/type checks.
- `CandidateFieldDefinition` wraps one full-field `ClosedBoundExpression`: scalar or complex for scalar codomains, or one dimension-checked fixed-vector expression for an `n`-component codomain. Candidate substitution and exact witness verification use this carrier rather than pretending T41's unary `MathematicalFunctionSpec` can encode `u(t,x)`.
- Temporal/spatial order analysis is derived from the AST. It supplies validation constraints but does not by itself prove which data are necessary/sufficient; equation class, locus, and well-posedness evidence remain explicit. It never rewrites the equation into a fake step rule.

### Side data and solution concept

- Side data use one closed `TraceRelation` schema: field values and/or evidenced derivative traces on a named, parameterized locus, plus a distinct periodic-locus identification. Unevidenced textbook boundary-name catalogs are not required. The structural constructor validates bound variables, locus membership, component/codomain, and derivative order; it does not guess whether the data make a problem admissible or well posed.
- Equation classification and locus analysis are explicit, versioned `EquationClassificationClaim`/analysis records with replayable premises or certificates and stated proof strength. A `SideDataAdmissibilityClaim` names those records, the exact side-data set, and either a versioned preset or analysis method. It may conclude valid, incompatible, underdetermined, overdetermined, or unknown; the conclusion is not embedded as a constructor heuristic.
- For the evidenced noncharacteristic evolutionary profiles, versioned preset `t45-cauchy-core-v1` may analyze a temporal-order-`m` Cauchy record carrying traces through order `m-1`, but only when its explicit equation-class/locus premises hold. Here diffusion uses `u(0,x)`, while wave/nonlinear profiles use `u(0,x)` and `u_t(0,x)`. This scoped preset is not promoted into a theorem for every PDE.
- The literal diffusion-plus-zero-velocity caption is recorded as incompatible redundant data rather than silently dropped or accepted.
- `Classical` is the only evidenced v1 solution-concept tag. One separate `DifferentialRegularity` record owns required derivative existence/continuity, avoiding contradictory concept/regularity identities. Weak, distributional, and other unevidenced concepts are deferred; an attempted request returns the generic unsupported-feature error rather than creating a public solution-concept token.
- Too little/much data, compatibility failure, and unknown well-posedness are typed problem-analysis results, not constructor guesses.

### No native transition state

- Native T45 has no source/frontier, neighborhood read, local result, commit, successor, or halting rule. It adds no update-algebra member.
- The immutable problem contains all information defining its solution set. A solver's mesh, time integrator state, nonlinear iteration, or residual history is algorithm work, not mathematical state.
- A well-posed IVP can derive a continuous flow/restriction operator. Its Cauchy state at time `tau` contains the field traces required by temporal order. This is a continuous relation whose existence/uniqueness scope must be proved or declared, not a sequence of T01/T44 events.
- A query at finitely many times evaluates/restricts a solution; sample count does not define evolution semantics.

### Query and result algebra

Closed requests include:

- candidate verification on a declared scope and solution concept;
- solve/model-set query with claim region and completeness target;
- point/restriction/sample evaluation of a referenced solution;
- existence, uniqueness, regularity, singularity, maximal-domain, domain-of-dependence, conservation, and stability properties;
- derived-flow or background-reduction claims with replayable premises.

Typed results include:

- `ExactSolutionWitness(candidate_field_id,scope,derivation_or_certificate)`;
- `ApproximateNumericalSolution(realization_id,interpolant_or_samples,error_claims)`;
- `MultipleSolutionWitness(distinct_verified_witnesses)`;
- `NoSolution(replayable_certificate)`;
- `UniqueSolution(certificate,scope,concept)`;
- `SingularityWitness` or bounded regularity failure;
- `Unknown`, `Unsupported`, `ResourceLimit`, `NumericalInstability`, `NonConvergent`, and `InvalidProblem`.

One witness proves existence only. Two distinct verified witnesses prove nonuniqueness only. Solver failure and bounded search prove neither nonexistence nor singularity. A finite sample cannot prove function equality.

### Numerical realization

```text
NumericalSolveRequest {
  problem_id,
  continuous_requested_region,
  domain_truncation,
  discretization_kind,
  mesh_and_stencils,

```

- Finite difference, finite element, method of lines, pseudospectral, symbolic/analytic, and future algorithms are versioned strategies, not branches in mathematical identity.
- A realization records every truncation, mesh/coordinate map, stencil/order, periodic/ghost lowering, initialization, integrator, arithmetic/literal conversion, tolerances, adaptivity, stopping/resource policy, and backend version that affects results.
- Result evidence separates residual/data error, consistency, stability, refinement/convergence, conservation, and replayably proved total-error claims. Courant satisfaction is neither convergence nor correctness; apparent convergence alone is not a proof.
- Work traces record nonlinear/linear iterations, adaptive steps, rejected steps, mesh refinements, residual histories, and resource use. They never feed back as hidden problem data.

### Native finite-difference relation

The Notes code normalizes to

```text
u[j,n+1] = 2*u[j,n] - u[j,n-1]
           + dt^2 * ((u[j-1,n]-2*u[j,n]+u[j+1,n])/dx^2 + f(u[j,n]))
```

`RotateLeft`/`RotateRight` prove a periodic finite discrete realization. `{u0,u1}` are two sampled time layers, not `(u,u_t)`. Equal Gaussian layers approximate zero velocity under this scheme. `NestList[...,400]` followed by `Map[First,...]` returns 401 initial-inclusive slices; `x=-20..20` by `.1` gives 401 spatial samples. These are exact properties of the related discrete experiment, not strict PDE topology.

### Identity and observations

- Structural equation identity: bound variables, field/codomain/dimension, equation/parameters, and numeric/operator versions.
- Problem identity: equation plus region, side data, regularity, and solution concept.
- Witness identity: problem plus multivariate candidate-field definition, claim scope, and proof/certificate provenance.
- Realization identity: problem/query plus all numerical choices and backend versions.
- Sample identity: referenced solution/result, coordinates, interpolation/evaluation semantics, numeric context, and errors.
- View identity: referenced sample/solution, coordinate orientation, projection/slice, range/tone/palette, crop/resampling, and labels.

Position-across/time-down plates, radial slices, absolute-value views, energy/residual plots, convergence grids, and apparent shocks are observers. They do not define the field, domain, condition, equation, solver, or correctness.
