- The source admits that details/shocks in the bottom complex plate may be discretization artifacts even though energy is roughly conserved. A finite raster is not a golden proof of a PDE solution.
- T41 closed function/operator syntax and query records, T31 constraint/solution-set discipline, and T44 continuous-field/numerical-realization responsibilities are likely reusable. Whether T45 has any native event/update algebra must be re-derived rather than assumed.

## Updated Assumptions

- Treat the immutable mathematical PDE problem—dependent fields, continuous domains, closed differential relation, parameters, initial/boundary data, regularity/solution concept—as distinct from any computed solution or trajectory.
- Do not assume every PDE is evolutionary, has a unique successor, or is pointwise classically defined. T45 may be a specification/query/solution category closer to T31/T41 than to the ten transition updates.
- A particular initial-value formulation may define a continuous flow when existence/uniqueness hypotheses hold; that relation is not a sequence of native discrete events and must not be serialized as T44 assignments.
- Initial conditions, boundary conditions, domain, equation, requested region, solution concept, solver, discretization, numerical realization, diagnostic work, sampled field, and rendering have independent identities.
- Equation order determines required data. First-order diffusion and second-order wave/nonlinear equations cannot share a blind `initial_field` record without derivative/order validation.
- Classical, weak, distributional, exact closed-form, certified numerical, and approximate sampled solutions remain distinct until evidence justifies a smaller set.
- Blowup, derivative singularity, nonexistence, nonuniqueness, unresolved solver work, instability, nonconvergence, and resource interruption need typed outcomes rather than NaNs, clipping, fake halts, or arbitrary solver output.
- Periodic boundaries in the two-Gaussian Notes experiment and rotations in the finite-difference implementation are explicit solve/discretization profiles; they do not silently define strict main topology.

## Preliminary Book Evidence

### E1 — Continuous field and differential rule

- Provenance: `BOOK:2022-2028`, strict main.
- Establishes: continuous space/time, a field value at every point/moment, and rate/derivative relations instead of explicit steps.

> “one supposes that there exists a definite gray level at absolutely every point in space and every moment in time”

> “Having no explicit time steps to work with, one must instead just specify the rate at which the gray level changes with time at every point in space.”

### E2 — Strict equations and data

- Provenance: `BOOK:2058-2078`, strict equations/caption.
- Establishes: named differential relations, derivative notation, display orientation, and initial data.

```text
diffusion:   partial_t  u(t,x) = (1/4) partial_xx u(t,x)
wave:        partial_tt u(t,x) =       partial_xx u(t,x)
sine-Gordon: partial_tt u(t,x) =       partial_xx u(t,x) + Sin[u(t,x)]
initial:     u(0,x)=Exp[-x^2], partial_t u(0,x)=0
```

The formulas are normalized transcriptions of extracted inline mathematics, not verbatim code.

### E3 — Equation need not yield a self-consistent future

- Provenance: `BOOK:2086-2094`, strict main.
- Establishes: formula sampling is not enough; values may diverge or vary infinitely fast, after which the equation cannot determine later behavior.

> “there is no guarantee that the equation will yield self-consistent results.”

> “the gray level one gets either becomes infinitely large or starts to vary infinitely quickly in space or time.”

### E4 — PDE as constraint and well-posed problem

- Provenance: `BOOK:13348-13358`, native Notes.
- Establishes: no built-in evolution, region/edge constraints, existence/nonuniqueness/inconsistency, and equation-class dependence.

> “PDEs do not have a built-in notion of ‘evolution’ or ‘time’.”

> “a PDE is essentially just a constraint on the values of a function at different times or different positions.”

> “If too little is specified, there may be many possible solutions, while if too much is specified there may be no consistent solution at all.”

### E5 — Discretization is not equation identity

- Provenance: `BOOK:13401-13412`, native Notes.
- Establishes: finite numerical approximations, Courant instability, and artifact risk.

> “To find numerical solutions to PDEs on a digital computer one has no choice but to make approximations.”

> “All however ultimately involve discretization”

> “it is often difficult to tell whether complexity that is seen is actually a consequence of the underlying PDE, or is instead merely a reflection of the discretization procedure.”
